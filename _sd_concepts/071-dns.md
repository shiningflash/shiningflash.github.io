DNS turns names humans can remember into addresses computers can route to. It looks simple until you ship something and a stale TTL on a recursive resolver costs you twelve minutes of downtime nobody can debug. This page will cover the resolution path (stub resolver → recursive → root → TLD → authoritative), the four layers of caching that decide how quickly a change actually reaches the user, what TTLs really do and why they are not a guarantee, the difference between `A`, `AAAA`, `CNAME`, `ALIAS`, and `SRV`, why DNS-based service discovery is fragile, and the failure modes that cost you when you do a cutover.
